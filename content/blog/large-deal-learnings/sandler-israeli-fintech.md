*Note: This is a deal I worked on at Hevo Data in 2023. All numbers and context shared with discretion.*

## The Context

In mid-2023, I was covering EMEA and APAC for Hevo Data - a Cloud ETL and Data Integration platform. We were in the $1M to $10M ARR journey as a company.

An Israeli fintech unicorn focused on cross-border payments reached out. They had recently gone through a merger and acquisition that tripled their data volumes overnight. Their existing data platform was not keeping up.

## Identifying the Pain

The first conversation was about understanding what was actually broken.

They mentioned three things:

- Lack of price transparency - they were being charged for historical imports they did not expect
- Real-time replication - cross-border settlements needed sub-10-minute data replication to stay compliant
- SLAs - their existing vendor's support had become unreliable at scale

The stated problem was volume. The real problem was trust - in their vendor, in the data, and in their own ability to operate at the new scale.

Peeling that onion took two conversations. You have to keep asking until you reach the layer that actually matters to their business.

## Building Trust

This deal did not happen over email.

It happened over WhatsApp.

The Head of Data and I exchanged over 100 messages across the deal cycle. Every concern surfaced fast. Every question got answered the same day. Formal channels would have added days to every loop.

Building real trust in a complex deal means being available in the way your buyer actually communicates - not the way that feels professional to you.

## Mapping the Buying Process

Early in the process I asked directly - how do you typically make decisions like this?

The answer: technical trial first, then pricing, then CEO sign-off.

There was also a hard deadline. If they did not decide within 3 months, they would be paying for two vendors simultaneously. That urgency was real and I treated it as shared urgency, not as pressure I was applying.

We built a joint action plan together mid-meeting. Not a vendor checklist - a shared document both sides owned. That changes the dynamic completely.

## Exec Connect

The Head of Data was our sponsor. His reputation was on the line.

At the right moment, our CRO reached out directly to their CEO. Not to push the deal - but to signal accountability at the highest level. That message landed because the timing was right and the relationship on the ground was already strong.

Exec connections only work when the deal below them is already solid.

## POC and Technical Alignment

We ran the POC across both QA and PROD environments, covering their US and EU data infrastructure.

We included Platinum Support in the proposal from the beginning - not as an upsell but as proof that we would be accountable after the signature. For a team burned by poor support, that mattered more than the feature list.

Technical alignment is not just about proving the product works. It is about proving you will be there when it does not.

## Pricing

Every direct ask about budget was deflected early in the process.

So I stopped asking directly. Instead I anchored every pricing conversation around two things:

- The cost they were already absorbing from unexpected charges
- The value of having reliable support at their new scale

When the number finally came up, it landed in context - not as a line item but as a solution to a problem they had already agreed was real.

## Solving the Anxiety

In October 2023 - one month before close - something shifted.

The Head of Data raised a concern that had nothing to do with the product. He was worried about our runway. Hevo was a growing startup and he was being asked to bet his infrastructure on us.

This is the anxiety nobody writes about in deal breakdowns.

We did not deflect it. Our CRO wrote directly to their CEO - addressing runway, investors, and long-term commitment. We put it on the table and answered it directly.

The deal closed in November 2023. I was on a WhatsApp voice note with the Head of Data when it did.

## How Sandler Mapped to Every Phase

Looking back, every step traces directly to the Sandler methodology - even when I did not realise it in the moment.

- **Pain identification** - M&A tripled volumes. Peeled the onion to find the real pain: unreliable support and price transparency.
- **Up-front contract** - Joint action plan built together. Clear next steps agreed at every stage.
- **Deeper pain** - Head of Data's reputation was on the line. That was the real stake.
- **Budget** - Led with cost savings and support value. Never asked directly.
- **Decision process** - Trial, pricing, CEO sign-off. Hard 3-month deadline. Urgency was real.
- **Fulfillment** - POC across QA and PROD. Platinum Support as proof of accountability.
- **Post-sell** - Vendor-size anxiety surfaced. Addressed it directly. Closed November 2023.

## Learnings

**Peel the onion.** The stated problem is rarely the real one. Keep asking until you reach the layer that actually matters to their business.

**Joint action plan is shared ownership.** Building it together mid-meeting creates commitment from both sides - not a one-sided vendor checklist.

**Informal channels build real trust.** Over 100 WhatsApp messages meant every concern surfaced fast and was addressed fast. Formal channels would have added days.

**Address the anxiety, not the price.** When a deal stalls late, the problem is rarely commercial. Find the emotional risk your buyer is carrying and address that first.

If you apply yourself and treat each conversation as something that can turn the deal - practise it, believe you can win - the method follows. The deal closed in November 2023. I was on a WhatsApp voice note with the Head of Data when it did.
